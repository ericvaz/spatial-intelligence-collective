The SIC Lab

A map, a toolbox, and open data.

Choose a variable and the Lab maps it across every district or municipality. The Calculator lets you wire variables into a composite index or drop in a method: spatial weights, global Moran's I, LISA clusters, Getis-Ord Gi* hot spots, the Gini and Theil inequality indices, and OLS and geographically weighted regression. Every result can go back onto the map, into the Composer as a finished map, or out as CSV, GeoJSON, or a shapefile.

  • India725 districts, 81 variables. Open the map, the Calculator and the Composer.
  • Brazil558 microregions, 46 variables. Open the map, the Calculator and the Composer.
  • Venezuela335 municipalities, 24 variables. Open the map, the Calculator and the Composer.

Tutorials

Spatial statistics, step by step.

Each lesson explains a method visually and then lets you run it in Python inside the page, first on teaching data and then on real census data from the Lab.

  • Spatial autocorrelation and Moran's IWhat it means for nearby places to resemble each other, how spatial weights are built, and how to compute and test global Moran's I.
  • LISA and hot-spot analysisLocal indicators of spatial association and Getis-Ord Gi*, for finding where clusters and outliers sit on the map.
  • Interpolation, variograms and krigingEstimating values between observations, from inverse distance weighting to the variogram and ordinary kriging.

Open data

Use the data in your own work.

Every variable in the Lab is a plain JSON file that can be read from a notebook, a script, or a web page, for example https://lab.spatialintelligence.network/v1/india/literacy_rate.json. The catalogue for each country is at /v1/india/meta.json, /v1/brazil/meta.json, and /v1/venezuela/meta.json. Sources and licences are listed with each file and in the Lab's Sources panel.
